## Design and reference comparison

Keep Niri 26.04's on-demand satellite policy. A pure-Go `internal/x11` owner
reserves a display lock and filesystem/abstract listening sockets, probes
`--test-listenfd-support`, and hands inherited descriptors to satellite on
readability. The compositor owns those sockets across child exits. Drain failed
connections before rearming so a broken executable cannot cause a restart loop.
Satellite connects to this compositor's Wayland socket; its xdg windows already
belong to the existing focus, layout, clipboard and capture paths.

References, at the pins in `docs/README.md`:

- Niri: `src/utils/xwayland/{mod,satellite}.rs`, `src/main.rs`,
  `niri-config/src/misc.rs`, and `src/niri.rs`'s `xwls_changed` path. Probe before
  publishing DISPLAY; remove the host DISPLAY when unavailable; configured
  environment takes precedence for ordinary children. Reload can enable a
  previously unavailable satellite and change the executable for its next start.
  Turning it off hides DISPLAY from new children without killing existing X11
  applications or removing established listeners.
- Mango: `src/main.c` creates lazy `wlr_xwayland`, then maintains X11-specific
  surface/XWM state. This conflicts with the selected satellite architecture.
- Hyprland: `src/xwayland/Server.cpp` reserves sockets and passes `-listenfd`,
  but `create()` says `TODO: lazy mode` and owns its own XWM. Reuse the descriptor
  handoff principle, not its eager startup or XWM.

Use Go's `net`, `os/exec`, and Linux `syscall` readiness support. Add no library.
Supervisor goroutines call no wlroots functions. Stop and reap the child and
release the owned sockets before destroying the compositor. Check shared `/tmp`
ownership, directory type and sticky permissions; never remove somebody else's
lock. Skip occupied display numbers rather than attempting stale-PID recovery,
as Niri does.
